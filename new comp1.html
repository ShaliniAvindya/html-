Competitor Analysis — LushWare Travel Tech SaaS

1. Executive Summary

1.1 Overall findings

FindingDetail
No highlighted competitor sells an agency's full operating systemEach of the seven owns a slice. TravelJoy and Travefy own the sales-and-proposal slice. Tern owns the approval-and-commission slice. Ezus and Tourwriter own trip production. TravelWorks owns mid/back office and accounting. Moonstride owns the broadest slice but only for larger operators. No one sells a self-serve platform that an agency can adopt as its operating backbone at SMB price.
The operation breaks at the seams between slicesThe recurring failure is handover: proposal to booking, booking to supplier, supplier to payment, payment to commission, commission to accounting, and finally back office to traveller. Agencies that buy two or three of these tools end up re-keying data at every seam — which is the exact cost LushWare is positioned to remove.
Nobody is self-serve past the entry tierTravelJoy and Travefy start self-serve, but Travefy's agency tools and CRM API are Contact Sales only. Ezus has no trial. Moonstride carries a setup fee from £1,500. TravelWorks is custom-quoted in some listings. The moment an agency grows past solo, the buying experience stops being SaaS and becomes enterprise sales.
AI is present but throttledTern has the widest AI footprint of the seven, and pauses every AI feature at 100% of a shared monthly allowance unless Tern Pro is added. TravelJoy ships a Copilot and Magic Email but has no public API. Travefy ships Smart Import only. Ezus, Tourwriter, TravelWorks and Moonstride do not lead with AI at all.
The traveller-facing end is the weakest link in the whole setOnly TravelJoy and Travefy have traveller apps, and both are criticised — one lacks live flight status, the other is view-only. The five operator and back-office platforms have no traveller app at all. In a business where the client experience is the product, this is the single largest unfinished area.
The commercial terms of the category are uniformly defensiveTrip caps (TravelJoy), annual-only printed pricing and paywalled support (Travefy), AI usage caps (Tern), no trial (Ezus), premium per-seat pricing (Tourwriter, TravelWorks), setup fees and opaque custom quotes (Moonstride). None of it is designed for an agency that wants to evaluate, adopt and scale on its own terms.

1.2 Important conclusions

  1. LushWare's category is not "travel CRM" — it is "agency operating system." Benchmarking against TravelJoy and Travefy as peers would understate the product and mis-set the price. The correct comparison set is every tool an agency currently runs its business on, combined.
  2. The wedge is the seam, not the feature. LushWare does not need to beat Travefy on itinerary beauty or Tourwriter on operator depth on day one. It needs to be the only platform where an agency never re-keys the same trip twice.
  3. Self-serve to mid-market is an unoccupied position. The market splits into self-serve-and-capped (TravelJoy, Travefy entry) and sales-led-and-expensive (Ezus, Tourwriter, TravelWorks, Moonstride). Nothing is self-serve and complete. That is LushWare's structural opening.
  4. Pricing should be per user, monthly, uncapped and setup-free, because every single friction identified across the seven competitors is a pricing or commercial-term friction, not a feature complaint.

1.3 Positioning statement

LushWare is the self-serve operating system for travel agencies. One platform where an agency runs the whole trip — enquiry, quote, itinerary, booking, supplier, payment, documents, traveller communication, commission and reporting — priced per user, monthly, with no trip caps, no setup fee and support included.

8.3 Three deliberate departures from the category

  1. Sell the operation, not a module. Every competitor sells a slice and expects the agency to integrate the rest. LushWare's pitch is that the agency stops integrating.
  2. Never let a commercial term interrupt the operation. No trip caps, no AI pauses, no paywalled support, no setup fees, no Contact Sales gate. Tern's AI cap and Travefy's support paywall are the two most exploitable weaknesses in the set.
  3. Ship the traveller app in the cheapest tier. Every competitor treats the traveller-facing end as an upmarket add-on or an afterthought. Making it standard is a positioning statement in itself.

9.1 Final answer

What should LushWare build, improve, or do differently to outperform its competitors?

Build the thing none of the seven has: a single system of record where a travel agency runs the entire trip — enquiry, quote, itinerary, booking, supplier, payment, documents, traveller communication, commission and reconciliation — delivered as self-serve multi-tenant SaaS with onboarding an agency can complete without a sales call.

Improve on the four operational failures that recur across the set: re-keying the same trip across systems (all seven), AI that stops working (Tern), traveller apps that do not work (Travefy, TravelJoy) and back-office accounting that lags the sales tooling (Tern, Travefy, TravelJoy).

Do differently commercially: per-user pricing at $29 / $49 / $79, available monthly, with no trip cap, no setup fee, no Contact Sales gate and support included in every tier — the direct inverse of Travefy's annual-only paywalled support, TravelJoy's 12-trip cap, Tern's AI allowance, Ezus's no-trial policy and Moonstride's £1,500 setup fee.

Target SMB travel agencies of 5–50 staff — the segment that has outgrown TravelJoy and Travefy but cannot justify Ezus, Tourwriter, TravelWorks or Moonstride, and that is currently running its business across two or three tools that do not talk to each other.
